How to Download Instagram Reels, Photos, and Stories Without a Watermark (Complete Guide)

Instagram doesn't provide a built-in download button for most content types — except for certain Reels that the creator has specifically allowed to be reshared. For saving educational videos, tutorials, or visual references offline, a separate extraction process from the post page is needed.

Content types you can download

  • Reels — short vertical videos, usually a few seconds to a few minutes long.
  • Single photos — regular photo posts at full resolution.
  • Carousels — posts with multiple photos/videos in one upload, marked with a grid icon in the corner.
  • Stories — temporary content active for 24 hours, as long as it's still available and the account is public.

Steps

  1. Open Instagram and find the post you want to download.
  2. Tap the three-dot icon in the top-right corner of the post, then choose Copy Link.
  3. Open the Instagram Downloader page and paste that link into the field provided.
  4. Click the Get Video button and wait a few seconds for the preview to appear.
  5. Click the download button under each result thumbnail — for carousels, every photo/video gets its own card and download button.

Why the result has no watermark

The username watermark is usually added as a separate overlay by the Instagram app itself during its own "save to device" process. When a video is pulled directly from its original source link (the exact file the video player on the post page plays), that extra watermark layer is never applied in the first place — the result is a clean video exactly as the creator uploaded it.

Reasonable limits

  • Private accounts can't be processed. This is purely Instagram's privacy policy — private account content is only meant for approved, logged-in followers.
  • Stories older than 24 hours (and not saved as a Highlight by the owner) are no longer active on Instagram's servers, so they can't be retrieved from any source.
  • Quality follows what the creator uploaded. If the original source was already low resolution, the downloaded result will follow that same resolution.

Etiquette around using downloaded content

Downloading for personal use (offline viewing, reference, archiving) is generally fine. If you plan to publicly reshare someone else's content, it's good practice to credit the original account or ask for permission first, especially for commercial use — this is a matter of content-sharing etiquette, not a technical limitation of the download process itself.
